EncCluster: Scalable Functional Encryption in Federated Learning through Weight Clustering and Probabilistic Filters

Encrypt fewer values. Keep federated updates protected.

Vasileios Tsouvalas · Samaneh Mohammadi · Ali Balador · Tanir Ozcelebi · Francesco Flammini · Nirvana Meratnia

PaperPublished versionCodeRepositoryPosterCite
OverviewMethodResultsCitation
The challenge

Encrypting every federated model weight can dominate clients’ compute time and upstream communication.

The contribution

EncCluster encrypts a small codebook of weight centroids and encodes their positions with Binary Fuse filters before secure aggregation.

Method overview · Based on the paper

The idea

At a glance

Secure aggregation can make federated learning expensive for devices with limited compute and bandwidth. EncCluster clusters model weights before functional encryption, then encodes the weight-to-cluster mappings with probabilistic filters, reducing the amount of data that must be encrypted and transmitted while allowing the server to reconstruct encrypted updates for aggregation.

  • The main efficiency gain comes from reducing the number of values encrypted to the centroid count.
  • Protecting centroid values and encoding their positions solve different problems; the server is intended to recover the mappings.
  • The no-filter variant is more compact, so the extra encoding has a visible communication cost that must be weighed against its narrower threat model.
  • Secure aggregation
  • Functional encryption
  • Weight clustering
Read the abstract

Federated Learning (FL) enables model training across decentralized devices by communicating solely local model updates to an aggregation server. Although such limited data sharing makes FL more secure than centralized approached, FL remains vulnerable to inference attacks during model update transmissions. Existing secure aggregation approaches rely on differential privacy or cryptographic schemes like Functional Encryption (FE) to safeguard individual client data. However, such strategies can reduce performance or introduce unacceptable computational and communication overheads on clients running on edge devices with limited resources. In this work, we present EncCluster, a novel method that integrates model compression through weight clustering with recent decentralized FE and privacy-enhancing data encoding using probabilistic filters to deliver strong privacy guarantees in FL without affecting model performance or adding unnecessary burdens to clients. We performed a comprehensive evaluation, spanning various datasets and architectures, to demonstrate EncCluster's scalability across encryption levels. Our findings reveal that EncCluster significantly reduces communication costs - below even conventional FedAvg - and accelerates encryption by more than four times over all baselines; at the same time, it maintains high model accuracy and enhanced privacy assurances.

The approach

How it works

  1. 01

    Cluster local model weights

    After local training, clients replace individual weight values with a compact set of cluster centroids and a mapping from weights to those centroids.

  2. 02

    Encrypt and encode

    Decentralized multi-client functional encryption protects the centroids. Binary Fuse filters compactly encode the weight mappings using shared seeds. This encoding is distinct from cryptographic encryption and is analyzed under the paper’s stated threat model.

  3. 03

    Reconstruct for secure aggregation

    The server queries the filters to recover the mappings and substitutes encrypted centroids for individual weights. Functional decryption then returns the aggregate model update.

Experiments & evidence

What the results show

Separate accuracy loss, upstream bytes, and client encryption time; efficiency results do not establish protection against every inference attack.

Data and models
CIFAR-10/100; ResNet-20, MobileNet, ConvNeXt-Tiny, and ConvMixer-256/8 trained from scratch.
Federated setting
Flower simulations; principal comparisons use 30 clients, 1 local epoch, 128 clusters, and 20% or 100% participation.
Compared with
FedAvg, FedAvg with weight clustering, DeTrust-FL, SEFL, and an EncCluster variant using Huffman coding instead of Binary Fuse filters.
Privacy evidence
Arguments for isolation/replay resistance and a cluster-reconstruction experiment measuring embedding MSE; no differential-privacy budget or general inference-attack bound.

Scope & limitations

  • The preprint assumes an honest-but-curious server and setup authority, secure channels, and protected key provisioning. Colluding clients may disclose weights but do not poison updates; denial-of-service and poisoning are excluded.
  • Binary Fuse filters are not encryption and the paper explicitly disclaims equivalent cryptographic guarantees. Their reconstruction errors are not an established differential-privacy guarantee.
  • With all other clients colluding, the aggregate can reveal a target’s clustered contribution. Lossy compression prevents exact recovery of original weights but does not establish absence of sensitive information leakage.
  • Evaluation uses two small vision datasets and reports client encryption time and upstream volume. It does not establish wall-clock, energy, server scalability, or deployed edge-device gains.
